Entrepreneurship—both as a word and a concept—has fascinated me since my undergraduate days studying Business Administration. This intrigue played a pivotal role in my decision to pursue a postgraduate degree in Entrepreneurship and Innovation at La Trobe University, Melbourne, Australia. However, I was unknowingly embarking on a journey with a far-reaching destiny. As I now reflect on pursuing a PhD, I find myself with over five years of work experience in academia as a lecturer and coordinator of a Business Incubation Program designed to foster entrepreneurship within a college setup. This blog documents my background, motivations, and vision as I strategize this next step in my career.

## The Accidental Entry into Academia

My entry into academia wasn’t a planned career move. Upon returning to Nepal from Australia, I sought opportunities to reconnect with the local context I had been detached from during my four years abroad. My initial goal was to understand these contextual factors and devise a career strategy, potentially to start a new venture. However, once I stepped into academia, there was no turning back. My personal life, external circumstances, and professional aspirations intertwined with this academic path, creating a truly phenomenal experience.

## Thriving in Challenging Roles

Despite being accidental, my role in academia turned out to be incredibly fulfilling. The diverse responsibilities left no room for monotony. An avid reader, critical thinker, and inherently curious individual, I found immense joy in seizing opportunities to satiate my curiosity. Whether it was through books, guest lectures, workshops, formal events, or panel discussions, each experience provided new perspectives and connections. These interactions often informed and inspired my work, fueling my growth as a professional and individual. Reflecting on the last five years, the journey has been nothing short of remarkable.

## The Question of Research

In academia, the adage “publish or perish” underscores the importance of research. My colleagues and I often deliberated on its significance in advancing teaching quality and academic reputation. While we organized professional development programs and workshops to enhance research skills, the demands of other responsibilities often left little time or motivation for meaningful research.

This realization was a turning point for me. If research is so critical, why was I unable to pursue it? Was it a lack of skills, time, or motivation? My exploration of PhD programs illuminated the gaps in my approach. The demanding nature of academic roles today requires continuous learning, adaptability, and technological acumen. I questioned whether I could sustain a meaningful career in academia without a PhD. The answer was clear: I needed to commit to this transformative journey, even if it meant transitioning from a full-time role to part-time teaching to prioritize my research ambitions.

## Entrepreneurship as a Discipline

Given my decade-long engagement with entrepreneurship, it is natural to consider it my academic discipline. However, I have come to learn that many universities view entrepreneurship not as a standalone discipline but as an interdisciplinary phenomenon. It intersects with various fields, including business, sociology, economics, and even engineering.

This perspective has broadened my understanding. I no longer see entrepreneurship merely as a field to validate what I already know but as a rich, multifaceted domain with endless opportunities for exploration. Its interdisciplinary nature draws from diverse theoretical frameworks, such as the Lean Startup methodology, and finds relevance in numerous research contexts—from pedagogy to marketing, finance, and law.

## Defining My Research Interests

The challenge now lies in narrowing my research focus. I have diverse interests but lack a unifying direction to guide my inquiries. While fields like business intelligence, generative AI, and analytics offer exciting future prospects, I think I cannot forgo the academic background and experience I have accumulated in the domain of entrepreneurship. I am still pondering about the research questions that are firstly impactful, and secondly interesting enough for the duration of PhD and beyond. My last one month of study and research has driven me this far and I hope I will soon solve this puzzle to take concrete steps towards a PhD Degree.
